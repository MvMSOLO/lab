5. THE SATISFYING PLAYGROUND

This is the heart of the application.

Create a visually stunning central interactive object, such as a liquid-glass orb floating above a subtle illuminated platform.

The object must respond to actual user input.

Interactions include:

- Tap: a soft radial ripple spreads from the touch point.
- Double tap: two overlapping waves produce a richer reaction.
- Press and hold: the orb slowly compresses and accumulates light.
- Release: it expands with a satisfying elastic rebound.
- Drag: the object follows the finger with smooth spring physics.
- Swipe: a wave of light travels through the scene.
- Rapid tapping: an increasingly complex but controlled sequence of ripples.
- Two-finger touch: a special symmetrical wave interaction.
- Pinch: the environment subtly changes scale or depth.
- Idle state: the orb slowly breathes and reflects ambient light.

Use actual touch coordinates. Avoid interactions that only play a canned animation regardless of where the user touches.

The physics must feel responsive, soft, and premium.

Use spring-like interpolation, damped motion, velocity-sensitive effects, and carefully tuned easing.

Never use harsh default browser animations or abrupt snapping.

All sound must be generated locally or derived from original synthesized sound design. Do not use copyrighted music or samples.

Create a sound palette including:

- Delicate ceramic clicks.
- Liquid droplets.

- Airy swishes.
- Subtle low-frequency thumps.
- Delicate metallic shimmer.
- Soft granular textures.
- Warm, short reverberant tails.
- Tiny high-frequency sparkles.

Different components should have different sound identities.

A glass button should not sound like a ceramic button. A liquid orb should not sound like a metal switch.

Dynamic audio response

Sound must respond to:

- Touch location.
- Tap intensity where measurable.
- Gesture speed.
- Hold duration.
- Release timing.
- Interaction type.
- The current environment.

Use gentle synthesized oscillators, filtered noise, envelopes, carefully controlled resonances, and short delays.

Use AudioContext, gain nodes, filters, and oscillators appropriately.

Avoid harsh sine-wave beeps, loud clicks, clipping, sudden volume spikes, and repetitive annoying sounds.

Implement a master volume slider, independent music and effects volume controls, and a global mute switch.

Respect mobile browser audio restrictions: initialize or resume audio only after a valid user gesture.

Prevent audio nodes and event listeners from accumulating during repeated interactions.
